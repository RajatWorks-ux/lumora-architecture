Villa Solara

A hillside villa cascading through terraced olive groves, framing panoramic views of the Val d'Orcia.

Typology Private Residence
Location Tuscany, Italy
Status Conceptual Design
Year 2024

Project Overview

Perched on a sun-drenched hillside in the heart of Tuscany's Val d'Orcia, Villa Solara is conceived as a continuation of the landscape itself — a residence that emerges from the terraced olive groves like a natural formation.

The design responds to the site's dramatic topography by stepping the villa down the slope in a series of cascading volumes. Each level opens to the valley below, framed by expansive glazing that dissolves the boundary between interior and the rolling Tuscan landscape. An infinity pool appears to spill into the valley, its edge calibrated to merge visually with the horizon.

Materiality is rooted in place: warm, honey-colored limestone quarried from the surrounding hills forms the primary facade, its surface bearing the subtle fossil records of the region's geological history. Travertine, sourced from nearby Rapolano, lines the pool and wet areas. Internally, white oak joinery and natural linen create a soft counterpoint to the stone's gravitas.

Design Concept

Architecture as topography

The concept emerges from a reading of the site as a constructed landscape — centuries of agricultural terracing have shaped this hillside into a series of horizontal platforms, each retaining olive trees and vineyards.

Villa Solara adopts this language of terracing. The residence is not a single volume placed on the land, but a series of connected pavilions that step with the slope. Each pavilion corresponds to a programmatic zone — living, sleeping, wellness, service — and each opens to its own private terrace, creating a sequence of indoor-outdoor rooms that cascade toward the valley.

The infinity pool is the project's central gesture: a horizontal plane of water inserted into the terracing logic, its edge disappearing into the Val d'Orcia. The pool terrace becomes the social heart of the villa, sheltered by a deep limestone soffit that frames the view while providing shade from the midday sun.

Below ground, a wine cellar carved into the hillside maintains a constant temperature naturally. A tasting room opens to a sunken courtyard, bringing light and air to the subterranean level. This interplay of above and below, heavy and light, solid and void, defines the spatial experience.
