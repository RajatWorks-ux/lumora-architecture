Casa Verde

A tropical sanctuary woven into the jungle canopy, where timber screens filter light and courtyards bring nature into every room.

Typology Private Residence
Location Tulum, Mexico
Status Conceptual Design
Year 2024

Project Overview

Nestled within the dense tropical forest of Tulum's Sian Ka'an biosphere reserve, Casa Verde is an exercise in radical indoor-outdoor living. The residence does not sit on the land — it grows within it.

The design strategy is one of subtraction: a series of courtyards carved from the built volume, each bringing light, air, and jungle into the heart of the home. Timber screens — crafted from sustainably harvested local zapote wood — filter harsh sunlight while allowing breezes to pass freely. The screens create a constantly shifting play of dappled light across stone floors.

Four bedroom suites are arranged around a central living courtyard, each with its own private garden. The main living space opens fully to a pool courtyard on one side and a dining courtyard on the other, creating a continuous flow between cooking, dining, lounging, and swimming — all immersed in vegetation.

Materials are deliberately primitive: chukum plaster walls (an ancient Mayan technique using tree resin), local limestone flooring, zapote wood screens and ceilings, and woven henequen textiles. The palette ages gracefully in the tropical climate, developing patina rather than deterioration.

Design Concept

Architecture as canopy

In the jungle, the canopy is the primary architectural element — filtering light, moderating temperature, creating habitat. Casa Verde translates this biological intelligence into architectural form.

The zapote wood screens function as a constructed canopy. Their spacing and orientation are parametrically calibrated to the sun path: dense at the west for afternoon shade, open at the east for morning light, transparent at the north for views. At night, the screens glow from within, becoming lanterns in the jungle.

The courtyard strategy creates a microclimate. Each courtyard is planted with specific species — one for fragrance (night-blooming jasmine), one for edibles (herbs, citrus), one for meditation (bamboo, moss), one for play (lawn, pool). The boundaries between courtyards are permeable, encouraging movement and discovery.

The roof is accessible and programmed: a stargazing terrace with a fire pit, an outdoor yoga platform shaded by a pergola, and a small observatory dome. The roof becomes a fifth facade — visible only to the birds and the stars.
