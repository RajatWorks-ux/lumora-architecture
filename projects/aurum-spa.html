Aurum Spa & Resort

A thermal wellness retreat integrating historic bathhouse architecture with contemporary spa design.

Typology Wellness Resort
Location Baden-Baden, Germany
Status Conceptual Design
Year 2024

Project Overview

Baden-Baden has been a destination for healing waters since Roman times. Aurum Spa & Resort continues this lineage, weaving a new chapter into the town's thermal heritage. The resort occupies a parkland site at the edge of the Black Forest, where twelve thermal springs emerge at temperatures between 34°C and 68°C.

The design strategy is one of careful insertion. A restored 19th-century bathhouse — the Friedrichsbad — becomes the historic heart, its ornate domed halls and Roman-Irish bathing ritual preserved intact. Around it, a series of contemporary pavilions unfold through the landscape: treatment villas, movement studios, meditation halls, and thermal pools both indoor and outdoor. The pavilions are clad in warm sandstone and timber, their forms echoing the region's agricultural vernacular.

Water is the project's protagonist. The thermal mineral water — rich in sodium, chloride, fluoride, and lithium — feeds a sequence of pools calibrated to different temperatures and purposes: a 36°C floating pool with underwater music, a 38°C vitality pool with massage jets, a 20°C plunge pool for contrast therapy, and a 34°C outdoor pool that steams in the Black Forest winter. Each pool is lined in hand-cut travertine, its warm tones complementing the water's natural translucence.

Design Concept

Water as architecture

The concept emerges from the water itself — its temperature, its mineral composition, its movement through the landscape. Architecture becomes a vessel for water's therapeutic properties.

The historic Friedrichsbad is treated with archaeological care: every dome, every column, every decorative tile is documented and restored. The Roman-Irish ritual — a sequence of hot air rooms, steam baths, soap-brush massage, and thermal pools — remains the spiritual center of the resort. New interventions are legible: steel and glass insertions are clearly distinguishable from historic fabric, yet harmonious in proportion and material tone.

The contemporary pavilions are arranged along a winding path through the park, each dedicated to a stage of the wellness journey: Arrival, Purification, Restoration, Integration. The path is not linear but circuitous, encouraging wandering and discovery. Pavilions are partially embedded in the land, their green roofs continuous with the park. Skylights and clerestories bring daylight deep into treatment rooms.

The outdoor thermal pool is the project's public gesture — a crescent of warm water opening to the Black Forest, its steam rising into the canopy. In winter, it becomes a luminous portal in the snow. The pool's travertine edge is heated, a subtle luxury for bare feet. This is wellness not as escape, but as reconnection — to water, to landscape, to the deep time of the earth's thermal heart.
