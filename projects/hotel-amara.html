Hotel Amara

A riad-inspired boutique hotel where traditional Moroccan craftsmanship meets contemporary luxury.

Typology Boutique Hotel
Location Marrakech, Morocco
Status Conceptual Design
Year 2024

Project Overview

Hidden behind an unassuming facade in the heart of Marrakech's historic medina, Hotel Amara reveals itself as a sequence of courtyards, each more enchanting than the last. The design reinterprets the traditional riad — the inward-facing Moroccan house organized around a central courtyard — for contemporary luxury hospitality.

The hotel comprises 24 suites arranged around three interconnected courtyards. The arrival courtyard welcomes guests with a reflecting pool and citrus trees. The central courtyard houses a swimming pool lined in hand-glazed zellige tiles, its water reflecting the carved cedar mashrabiya screens above. The garden courtyard offers a quiet retreat with a fountain, herb gardens, and views toward the Atlas Mountains.

Every surface tells a story of craft: tadelakt plaster walls polished to a luminous sheen, zellige tilework in geometric patterns drawn from Almohad architecture, carved cedar doors and screens, hand-forged bronze lanterns and hardware. These traditional techniques are deployed with contemporary restraint — patterns are simplified, color palettes muted to warm neutrals, allowing the craft to speak without ornament overwhelming space.

Design Concept

The riad reimagined

The traditional riad is an architecture of concealment and revelation — a blank facade gives way to an inner sanctuary of light, water, and greenery. Hotel Amara amplifies this sequence across three courtyards, each with its own character and microclimate.

The arrival courtyard is public and transitional — a moment of decompression from the medina's intensity. A single plane of water reflects the carved stone portal, while bitter orange trees scent the air. The central courtyard is the social heart: a generous pool lined in emerald zellige, surrounded by arched galleries that lead to guest suites. Mashrabiya screens filter the harsh Moroccan sun, casting geometric shadows that move across the water throughout the day.

The garden courtyard is private and contemplative — a quadripartite garden in the Persian tradition, with a central marble fountain, rosemary and lavender hedges, and a pergola draped in bougainvillea. Here, the only sounds are water and birdsong.

The rooftop terrace crowns the experience: a 360-degree panorama over the medina's terracotta roofs to the Koutoubia minaret and, on clear days, the snow-capped Atlas. A fire pit and outdoor bar extend the season into the cool desert nights.
